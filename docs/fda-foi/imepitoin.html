  • Pexion™ (NADA/ANADA 141-509)

Pexion™ NADA 141-509, Original approval, December 04, 2018; sponsor Boehringer lngelheim Animal Health USA, Inc.; species: Dogs; foi_id 5907; FDA PDF

Indication

Treatment of noise aversion in dogs

Dose regimen

30 mg/kg (13.6 mg/lb), oral (tablet), twice daily, approximately 12 hours apart, Initiate 2 days prior to the day of the expected noise event and continue through the noise event

PexionTM should be administered orally at a dose of 13.6 mg/lb (30 mg/kg) of body weight twice daily, approximately 12 hours apart using a suitable combination of whole and half tablets. Initiate therapy starting 2 days prior to the day of the expected noise event, and continuing through the noise event.

Target-animal safety

dose multiples 1X (30 mg/kg twice daily), 3X (90 mg/kg twice daily), 5X (150 mg/kg twice daily); duration 6 months (26 consecutive weeks), twice daily oral, fasted, masked GLP; animals 32 (8 per group).

Study Animals: 32 healthy Beagle dogs, approximately 5.5 months of age and 4.9 to 7.3 kg body weight, 8 dogs per group (4 males and 4 females) Treatment Groups and Drug Administration: Imepitoin was administered as an oral tablet twice-daily at approximately 12-hour intervals for 6 months (26 consecutive weeks).
FindingQuote
Sedation signs (decreased activity, relaxed nictitating membranes) in all groups, dose-dependent; eyelid closure and ataxia only at 5XSigns of sedation: Decreased activity and relaxed nictitating membranes were seen in all groups and increased in frequency as the dose increased. Eyelid closure and ataxia were seen in 5X dogs only.

Effectiveness

Multi-site (Germany, Netherlands) randomized 1:1, masked, vehicle-controlled GCP field study (study 2015385) using New Year's Eve fireworks as the noise event; 251 randomized, 238 treated (114 imepitoin, 124 vehicle); 30 mg/kg twice daily from 2 days before through the event; n = 193 evaluable (89 imepitoin, 104 control); primary endpoint: Co-primary: owner-rated overall treatment effect vs previous untreated years (excellent/good/some/no/worse) and sum of 16 Lincoln Sound Sensitivity Scale behavior scores at four fixed time points; result: Good/excellent effect 59/89 imepitoin vs 26/104 control (odds ratio 5.048, 95% CI 2.64-9.66, p=0.0001); behavior-score treatment difference -6.8 (95% CI -10.1 to -3.5), p=0.0004

One hundred and ninety-three (193) animals (89 treated and 104 control) were evaluated for effectiveness. For the first co-primary variable of owner assessed overall treatment effect, the proportion of dogs with good or excellent treatment effect was higher in dogs treated with imepitoin (59/89 dogs) than in those administered control product (26/104 dogs).

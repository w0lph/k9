Panacur® C (OTC) Safe-Guard® Canine (OTC) Panacur® Granules 22.2% (Rx) NADA 121-473, Supplemental approval, March 19, 2002; sponsor Intervet, Inc.; species: Dogs; foi_id 381; FDA PDF

Indication

Treatment and control of roundworms, hookworms, whipworms and tapeworms (Taenia pisiformis) in adult dogs (including pregnant bitches) and puppies six weeks of age or older.

Dose regimen

50 mg/kg (22.7 mg/lb) body weight daily, Oral, mixed with a small amount of the usual food, Once daily, Three (3) days in a row; packets of 1 g, 2 g and 4 g dosed by body weight

The daily dose for PANACUR®-C is 50 mg/kg (22.7 mg/lb.) of body weight.

Extraction notes: Supplemental approval (2002) changing the 22.2% granule packets from Rx to OTC status (PANACUR-C). The summary contains no studies: effectiveness and safety are stated to be unaffected and are cross-referenced to the original approval dated 27 October 1983. Agency conclusions note that the original Rx status reflected a hepatotoxicity concern and that post-approval marketing experience has not shown a pattern of liver disease. No PK, TAS or adverse-reaction data.

Panacur® C (OTC) Safe-Guard® Canine (OTC) Panacur® Granules 22.2% (Rx) NADA 121-473, Supplemental approval, May 31, 1994; sponsor Intervet, Inc.; species: Felidae and Ursidae; foi_id 1615; FDA PDF

Indication

Control of internal parasites (ascarids Toxocara cati/Toxascaris leonina, hookworms Ancylostoma spp., tapeworms Taenia spp. as listed per species) in wild Felidae (lion, tiger, cheetah, puma, panther, leopard, jaguar) and Ursidae (grizzly, polar and black bears).

Dose regimen

10 mg/kg/bw daily for 3 days, Orally, mixed with food or as a top dressing, Daily, 3 days

Felidae and Ursidae: 10 mg/kg/bw daily for 3 days

Target-animal safety

dose multiples 5X, 10X; duration Felidae dosed for six (6) days; polar bears for four (4) days.

Five big cats, including two jaguars, one puma, one lion, and one Siberian tiger representing both sexes and ranging in age from 2 to 15 years of age were dosed with the drug. Additionally, three polar bears ranging in age from 7 to 9 years of age and representing both sexes were dosed with 100 mg/kg/bw of the drug for four (4) days. The Felidae were dosed with 100 mg/kg/bw for six (6) days.
FindingQuote
Periods of inappetence in one jaguar and the lion after initial consumption at ten times the recommended level (extreme ambient heat noted as a possible factor)In one jaguar and the lion, there were periods of inappentence (sic) following the initial consumption of fenbendazole at ten times the recommended level.
No adverse reactions at exaggerated doses; only remarkable finding was possible refusal of the drugThe only remarkable finding is that the overdosed animal may not consume the drug.
Safety parameters limited to clinical observation; no necropsiesDue to the nature of the study, safety parameters were limited to those which could be obtained from clinical observation of the animals.

Effectiveness

Six pivotal zoo field trials (three in Felidae with 29 animals, three in Ursidae with 26 animals) using each animal as its own control by comparing pre- and post-treatment fecal ova counts; three corroborative trials; no statistics applied; primary endpoint: Reduction in fecal parasite ova counts post-treatment; result: 100% reduction in Ancylostoma and Taenia spp. ova where observed; 100% reduction in ascarid ova in most bear studies and 80% in one (bears dosed 2 days, hence 3-day label); pronounced pre/post difference in greater than 90% of animals tested

There are three studies involving 29 members of the family Felidae and three studies involving 26 members of the family Ursidae.

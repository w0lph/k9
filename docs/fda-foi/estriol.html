Products

  • Incurin® (NADA/ANADA 141-325)

Incurin® NADA 141-325, Original approval, July 25, 2011; sponsor Intervet, Inc.; species: Dog, ovariohysterectomized females; foi_id 882; FDA PDF

Indication

Control of estrogen-responsive urinary incontinence in ovariohysterectomized female dogs

Dose regimen

2 mg per dog (2 tablets), not dependent on body weight, Oral, Once per day, Initial dose for a minimum of 14 days; then reduce step-wise (2 mg, 1 mg, 0.5 mg once daily, with at least 7 days between adjustments) to the lowest effective daily dose, which may be given once every two days; never more than 2 mg/day; reassess diagnosis if no response to 2 mg/day

All dogs should receive an initial dose of 2 mg INCURIN Tablets (2 tablets) orally once per day for a minimum of 14 days.

Target-animal safety

dose multiples 1X (2 mg/dog), 3X (6 mg/dog), 5X (10 mg/dog); duration 26 weeks, once daily; animals 4 treatment groups of 6 dogs each (twenty four).

Animals: Twenty four healthy, purebred female ovariohysterectomized Beagle dogs approximately 1 year of age weighing 7.3 to 10.5 kg at the beginning of the treatment period were allocated to 4 treatment groups of 6 dogs each. Test Material: Single scored tablets containing 1 mg of estriol. Dosage: 0, 2, 6, and 10 mg of estriol per dog (0, 1X, 3X, 5X, respectively) administered once daily for 26 weeks.
FindingQuote
All estriol-treated dogs developed vulvar discharges vs 2 of 6 placebo dogsAll dogs in the estriol-treated groups developed vulvar discharges (often reported daily) over the course of the study, whereas only 2 of 6 placebo-treated dogs did (a single observation in 1 dog and 2 observations in a second placebo group dog).
Mammary hyperplasia in 5/6 dogs at 2 mg and all dogs at 6 and 10 mg (1 placebo dog)Mammary hyperplasia was reported in 1 dog treated with placebo (2 observations), 5 of 6 dogs treated with 2 mg (2 observations for 1 dog and multiple observations for 4 dogs), and all dogs treated with 6 mg and 10 mg of estriol per day (multiple observations).
Higher WBC, absolute neutrophil and platelet counts at 6 and 10 mg/day than placeboDogs treated with 6 mg and 10 mg of estriol per day had higher white blood cell counts, absolute neutrophil counts, and platelet counts than dogs that received placebo.
No estriol-related bone marrow changes at 10 mg/day (M:E ratios comparable to placebo)There were no estriol-related changes on bone marrow smears evaluated microscopically from dogs in the 10 mg group
Kidney: increased lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate and urothelial hyperplasia of the pelvis only in estriol-treated dogs (low incidence, not dose-related)In the kidney, increased lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate in the pelvis and hyperplasia of the urothelium lining the pelvis were only observed in dogs treated with estriol; these findings were thought to represent an inflammatory reaction in the pelvis of the kidney.
Conclusion: acceptable margin of safety up to 10 mg/dog/day (5X) for 26 weeks; estrogenic effects associated with estriol(5 times the proposed maximum daily dose) for 26 weeks to healthy, ovariohysterectomized female dogs approximately 1 year of age had an acceptable margin of safety. Estrogenic effects (swollen vulva, vulvar discharge, mammary hyperplasia) were associated with the administration of estriol.

Effectiveness

Multi-site (27 sites), randomized, masked, placebo-controlled field study in client-owned ovariohysterectomized female dogs >=1 year with >=3 incontinence episodes/week (Study No. 2024-006-00): 14-day effectiveness phase (2 mg estriol once daily vs placebo) followed by a 28-day unmasked titration phase on estriol; 226 enrolled (115 estriol, 111 placebo; safety population), 206 evaluable for effectiveness; n = 106 estriol, 100 placebo (effectiveness analysis); primary endpoint: Treatment Success at Day 14: Investigator Response-to-Treatment score of continent or improved (Responder) AND a targeted reduction in weekly urinary incontinence episodes vs baseline (owner diary); result: Day 14 Treatment Success 66% (70/106) with estriol vs 37% (37/100) with placebo, P = 0.0038. Responders 73.6% vs 53%; target episode reduction met by 69.8% vs 40%. During titration, 51% of 191 dogs were controlled on less than 2 mg/day.
